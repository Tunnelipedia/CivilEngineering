On wet vs. dry: M and N now genuinely differ between the wet and dry cases, and they should — this was corrected after review. The reason is buoyancy, not the water pressure itself: submerging the ground reduces its effective unit weight, which reduces the effective overburden (and hence the deviatoric stress σ′v−σ′h that drives bending), for the same K. So a wet case can show a genuinely lower moment than a dry one, even though it also carries extra hydrostatic thrust. The one thing that is still strictly true is narrower than "water doesn't affect M": a uniform hydrostatic pressure, added on its own with the effective stress state held fixed, contributes zero moment (pure hoop thrust, by thin-ring theory) — that part only shows up here as the N-only water term below, not as a reason the two cases should match overall.

Critical Force Combinations (for M–N section design)

Because M(θ) and N(θ) are both pure A + B·cos2θ functions, their true extremes occur exactly at θ = 0°/180° (springlines) and 90°/270° (crown/invert) — found here analytically. Each card below is a single, self-contained load case (one interface condition, one water condition); "Corr." values are read at the same θ as the extreme, within that same case.

Values Around the Ring (every 30°)

Method note: θ is measured counter-clockwise from the right springline (θ = 0°); crown = 90°, left springline = 180°, invert = 270°. Formulas: Q2 = (E₀/El)·[1/(1+ν)]·[Ro³/(12·Ieff)]; No-slip ("Full Shear") Mmax = (P₀Ro²/4)/[1+Q₂(3−2ν)/(3−4ν)], Nvar = (P₀Ro/2)/[1+2νQ₂/((3−4ν)(1+Q₂))]; Full-slip ("No Shear") Mmax = (P₀Ro²/2)·(3−4ν)/(5−6ν+4Q₂), Nvar = (P₀Ro/2)·(3−4ν)/(5−6ν+4Q₂); constant hoop force N₀ = PavRo (both cases); P₀ = σ′v(1−K), Pav = σ′v(1+K)/2, evaluated separately for the wet and dry σ′v. Hydrostatic thrust Nw = PwRo is added to N only, for the wet case only. This elastic-continuum closed form assumes a deep tunnel (uniform far-field stress across the ring), a "wished-in-place" lining, and a homogeneous, isotropic, linear-elastic ground and lining — it does not capture staged construction, ground non-linearity, layered strata, or explicit radial-joint articulation (only the smeared stiffness-ratio approximation above). Use it as a preliminary/cross-check tool, not a substitute for a validated bedded-beam-spring or FE model for final design.

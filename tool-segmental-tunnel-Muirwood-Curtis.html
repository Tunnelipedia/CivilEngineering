Bending Moment — Ring Diagrams

Values in kNm/m · positive = per source formulation (see method note)
Why the Full water head and Dry diagrams look identical for a given interface: water pressure is a uniform radial load. By thin-ring theory, a uniform radial pressure produces pure hoop thrust — it has no eccentricity and therefore contributes exactly zero bending moment. So M(θ) is genuinely, mathematically independent of the water condition; only the axial thrust N(θ) changes between wet and dry. This is expected behaviour, not a calculation error — both diagrams are still shown per case for direct side-by-side comparison with the thrust diagrams below.

Critical Force Combinations (for M–N section design)

Because M(θ) and N(θ) are both pure A + B·cos2θ functions, their true extremes occur exactly at θ = 0°/180° (springlines) and 90°/270° (crown/invert) — found here analytically, not by numerical search. "Corr." values are read at the same θ as the extreme, for direct use in M–N interaction/section checks.

Method note: θ is measured counter-clockwise from the right springline (θ = 0°); crown = 90°, left springline = 180°, invert = 270°. This elastic-continuum closed form assumes a deep tunnel (uniform far-field stress across the ring), a "wished-in-place" lining, and a homogeneous, isotropic, linear-elastic ground and lining — it does not capture staged construction, ground non-linearity, layered strata, or explicit radial-joint articulation (only the smeared stiffness-ratio approximation above). Use it as a preliminary/cross-check tool, not a substitute for a validated bedded-beam-spring or FE model for final design.
